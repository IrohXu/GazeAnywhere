Agentic Gaze Analysis with AR Interaction

Houze Yang  ·  Xu Cao  ·  Chen Fang  ·  Inki Kim  ·  James Matthew Rehg

Core model: GazeAnywhere — accepted to CVPR 2026 (main conference)

Social gaze—where a person looks, and at whom, during face-to-face interaction—provides an important window into social cognition. Atypical gaze behavior is a well-established marker of autism spectrum disorder and related neurodevelopmental conditions, but measuring it rigorously in clinical settings remains challenging. Manual annotation from video recordings is labor-intensive, subjective, and difficult to scale. As a result, objective gaze metrics are still hard to obtain in both clinical practice and behavioral research, especially in naturalistic, real-world settings.

GazeAgent addresses this challenge through an end-to-end platform designed for pediatric clinical studies. During a session, AR glasses continuously capture egocentric video. GazeAnywhere, a promptable gaze model accepted to CVPR 2026, estimates gaze targets in real time from natural-language subject descriptions, avoiding reliance on brittle detection pipelines. Qwen builds a scene vocabulary during capture, and SAM 3 with SigLIP2 ground it into named objects after the session, associating gaze locations with entities in the environment. Finally, a Claude-based reasoning agent supports open-ended post-session queries through voice interaction, enabling clinicians and researchers to move more directly from recorded behavior to interpretable, actionable insights.

System Pipeline

Real-Time Capture + Post-Session Query

GazeAgent decouples capture from analysis. During recording, the AR glasses stay passive and stream egocentric frames to an on-premise backend, where GazeAnywhere and Qwen run continuously in the background — so the heavy computation is front-loaded and never enters the clinician's field of view. The expensive grounding step (SAM 3 + SigLIP2) runs only once, on the first query, to finalize precise gaze–object attribution. The Claude code agent then answers open-ended voice queries over the resulting structured gaze timeline, because clinically, knowing what the child looked at matters as much as knowing where.

Perception Backbone

GazeAnywhere: Promptable Gaze Target Estimation CVPR 2026

GazeAnywhere introduces Promptable Gaze Target Estimation (PGE) — a new end-to-end, concept-driven paradigm for gaze analysis. Instead of relying on brittle multi-stage pipelines that require explicit head bounding boxes or pose estimation, GazeAnywhere conditions gaze prediction directly on flexible text or visual prompts, integrating subject localization with gaze estimation in a single forward pass.

GazeAnywhere

End-to-End Concept-Driven Gaze Estimation

Given an image and a natural-language prompt like "the boy in the blue shirt", GazeAnywhere simultaneously localizes the subject's head, predicts a 2D gaze target heatmap, and classifies whether the gaze target is in or out of frame.

The architecture uses frozen DINOv3 and dino.txt encoders to extract visual and text features, projected into a shared embedding space. A transformer-based detector fuses these representations and feeds three specialized decoders: a Gaze Tracker (heatmap), a Head Tracker (bounding box), and a Presence Predictor (in/out classification).

Why Promptable?

Prior gaze models require pre-detected head bounding boxes as input, creating a sequential dependency on external detectors (OVDs). Errors in detection cascade into gaze estimation failures — especially in crowded scenes and with children's faces.

GazeAnywhere eliminates this bottleneck. A text prompt like "the boy in the blue shirt" is all the model needs to identify the subject and estimate gaze, end-to-end.

SOTA Results

GazeAnywhere achieves state-of-the-art on all PGE benchmarks: GazeFollow-Concept, VAT-Concept, ChildPlay-Concept, and the out-of-domain Child-SC clinical dataset. It outperforms all two-stage OVD+Gaze pipelines while being faster and more robust.

Text prompting matches visual prompting performance — appearance and pose descriptions are the most critical prompt components.

Supporting Models

Scene Understanding & Grounding

To turn a raw gaze point into what the child looked at, three on-premise vision models run as a coarse-to-fine chain: Qwen names the scene, SAM 3 localizes it, and SigLIP2 binds the two together. Each stage consumes the previous stage's output.

Step 1 · Naming

Qwen 3.5-27B

Visual Scene Comprehension (local VLM)

Processes key frames every 2 s as a strict salient-entity summarizer, emitting hierarchical labels: broad categories (e.g. "toy") plus detailed descriptions (e.g. "blue toy dinosaur").

Feeds → Broad categories to SAM 3, detailed phrases to SigLIP2
Step 2 · Localizing

SAM 3

Object Tracking & Segmentation

Takes Qwen's broad categories and runs in video mode, extracting temporally consistent masks that are converted into stable, tracked bounding boxes — reliable spatial anchors, but unlabeled at the fine-grained level.

Depends on ← Qwen  |  Feeds → Tracked per-object boxes to SigLIP2
Step 3 · Binding

SigLIP2

Vision-Language Matching Bridge

Closes the loop: for each SAM 3 box, it scores Qwen's detailed descriptions within that category and assigns the best-matching phrase — resolving multi-object ambiguity that SAM 3's coarse masks leave open.

Depends on ← Qwen + SAM 3 Final phrase → bounding-box grounding
Agent Design

Multi-Stage LLM Agent with Controlled Execution

Not a single prompt-response call. The agent runs as a staged pipeline — Formatter → Code Generator → sandboxed execution → Naturalizer — that decomposes each request into a validated JSON spec, generates a single executable Python function over the structured gaze timeline, runs it in a locked-down sandbox, and converts the result back into clinician-facing language. It uses no external orchestration framework like LangChain.

1

Formatter

Claude Sonnet 4.6

Parses free-form voice input into a strict JSON task spec. An ethical-refusal rule maps subjective constructs (e.g. "Was the child engaged?") to status: "ambiguous" with a concrete reformulation, so the system never hallucinates inner-state measurements.

2

Code Generator

Claude Sonnet 4.6

Emits a single run_task(context) Python function from a curated catalogue of allowed helpers and seven few-shot templates. It skips chain-of-thought to cut latency, composing analysis logic only.

3

Sandbox Runtime

Constrained Python

The host strips markdown fences, compiles with compile(), and runs via exec() in an isolated namespace where __builtins__ is emptied — blocking eval, import, and open.

4

Naturalizer

Claude Haiku 4.5

Maps the structured execution dictionary into a concise reply using predefined response templates — closing the loop from voice question to spoken answer.

Interaction Design

AR Glasses as the Primary Interface

The clinician wears AR glasses throughout the session, keeping full attention on the child. Recording is started and stopped via a physical button on the device — deliberately avoiding voice for this step, so the clinician can prepare without disturbing the patient.

All analysis commands are issued by voice. Spoken queries are transcribed server-side via ASR and routed to the agent. After the session, the same interface supports replay queries, trajectory review, and behavioral analysis.

Supported query categories
Entity & eye contact

Whether and what the child attended to — environmental objects or the interactor — as boolean checks, timing, and accumulated duration.

Macro-behavioral profiling

A holistic gaze-shift timeline summarizing the chronological sequence of attention allocation across the session.

Sequential & relational logic

Temporal dependencies between events — what the child looked at before or after a cue, gaze shifts, and ordering of fixations.

Temporal slicing

Any query can be scoped to a phase of the interaction (e.g. the first 30 seconds, or a 10–45 s window).

Boundary defense

Ambiguous or out-of-scope requests are met with a clarification prompt or refusal — never a hallucinated answer.

Setup 2 · Clinical Recordings

Example Visualization on Children Interaction Videos

Unlike egocentric capture, third-person recordings of standardized child–clinician interactions are permitted under our IRB protocol. We apply GazeAnywhere's gaze-target grounding to sessions from two clinical evaluation protocols — ESCS (Early Social Communication Scales) and BOSCC (Brief Observation of Social Communication Change). Driven only by free-form text prompts (e.g. "blue dinosaur toy," "woman with glasses," "xylophone"), the underlying vision-language model performs fine-grained visual-semantic alignment, precisely locating unstructured objects in cluttered clinical scenes — or correctly predicting out-of-scene attention shifts.

Annotation key The head bounding box encodes where the child is looking — red: gaze falls outside the frame and not on the interactor; blue: gaze on the interactor; green: gaze somewhere in the scene. The green dot marks the predicted gaze point, and the yellow box marks the localized gaze target object.
